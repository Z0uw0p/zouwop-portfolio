Wayfinding and a new public face for a city archive.

Brief

A city archive moving into a new home needed a wayfinding system that could guide researchers, school groups and first-time visitors through the same doors without turning the building into a series of signs.

Approach

We treated the building as a map rather than a corridor of arrows: each floor got its own colour, number and grid position, and the signage carried the same language from the street through to the reading rooms. The visual identity was kept deliberately quiet so the collection itself stayed the loudest thing in the room.

Outcome

The archive opened to the public with a navigation system staff could extend themselves as floors reopened. Visitor feedback picked out the colour-coded floors as the feature that made the building legible, and the identity now appears on everything from the website to the reading-room cards.
